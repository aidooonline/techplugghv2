Why the EliteBook wins on value

A new MacBook is a lovely machine, but it costs many times what a UK-used business laptop does. For the price of one new MacBook Air you could buy several tested EliteBooks. The trap is the cheap used MacBook: the GHS 2,000 to 4,000 units on the local market are usually 2014 to 2017 Intel MacBook Airs with 128GB of storage and macOS versions that Apple no longer fully supports. Next to that, a UK-used EliteBook at GHS 2,500 gives you a bigger 256GB SSD, RAM you can upgrade to 32GB, and Windows 11 with full Microsoft Office, which is what most offices, schools and businesses in Ghana actually run.

When a MacBook does make sense

A MacBook is the right call if you are already in the Apple ecosystem (iPhone, iCloud, iMessage), if you do macOS-specific creative work like Final Cut Pro or Logic, or if you simply prefer macOS and have the budget for a recent model. The current Apple M-series MacBooks are genuinely fast and have superb battery life. Just buy a recent one new or lightly used, not a decade-old Intel model, and go in knowing you are paying a premium for the brand and the OS.
