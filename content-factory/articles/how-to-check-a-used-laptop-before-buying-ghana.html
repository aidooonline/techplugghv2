1. Confirm the specification

Open Settings, then System, then About. Confirm the processor, installed RAM and Windows edition match what the seller advertised. This is the fastest way to catch a unit that does not have the specification claimed. On an EliteBook you should see a Core i5, the stated RAM, and Windows 10 or 11 Pro.

2. Test the battery health

Battery is the part of a used laptop that ages most, so this check matters more than any other. A good seller will already know and disclose the battery health as a percentage of the original design capacity. You can also generate a battery report yourself in a couple of minutes. Treat a seller who cannot or will not tell you the battery health as a warning sign.

3. Check the storage

Confirm the laptop has an SSD, not an old mechanical hard drive, and that the capacity matches the listing. An SSD is the single biggest reason a laptop feels fast. Every EliteBook TechPlug GH sells has a 256GB NVMe SSD, health-scanned before listing. If a used laptop still boots slowly, it may have a failing drive.

4. Inspect the screen

Display a full white image and then a full black image on the screen. White reveals dead or stuck pixels and dark spots; black reveals backlight bleed and bright spots. Minor marks may be acceptable at a lower grade, but you should know about them before you pay.

5. Test the keyboard and ports

Open a text document and press every key to confirm none is dead or sticky. Then test the ports: plug into each USB-A and USB-C port, the HDMI output, and the headphone jack. On an EliteBook, also check that the fingerprint reader works if you plan to use it.

6. Check the webcam and audio

Open the Camera app to confirm the webcam works, then play a video or music to test the speakers, and record a short clip to test the microphone. These matter if you take video calls for work or study.

7. Confirm it is genuine and activated

Check that Windows is activated in Settings, and make sure the laptop is not locked with a BIOS password or an account you cannot access. A properly refurbished unit is wiped and reinstalled cleanly, with no previous owner's data or locks. This protects you from buying a machine that turns out to be locked or improperly sourced.
