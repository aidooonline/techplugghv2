What affects a laptop's price in Ghana

Four things move the price most. Condition and origin: new costs far more than UK-used, and tested and graded stock is worth more than untested. Specification: more RAM, a bigger SSD, an i7 over an i5, or workstation graphics all raise the price. Model class: a business laptop such as an EliteBook holds value better than a consumer model. And the cedi-to-dollar exchange rate, since almost all stock is imported, so prices can shift with the currency. Buying a well-specified business laptop in the value band protects you against most of this.

How much RAM and storage do you actually need?

For office work, study, browsing and video calls, 8GB RAM and a 256GB SSD are enough, and that is what most of the value-band EliteBooks ship with. If you keep dozens of browser tabs open, run virtual machines, or do heavier multitasking, pay a little more for 16GB. The single biggest speed upgrade, though, is the SSD itself, which every EliteBook here already has. For a deeper look, read 8GB vs 16GB RAM.

Bottom line

Expect to pay GHS 2,500 to 3,500 for a laptop that does serious daily work well in Ghana. In that band, a tested UK-used HP EliteBook gives you business-class build and speed that a new laptop at the same price cannot match. Spend less and you compromise on power or condition; spend much more only if you need the newest hardware or heavy creative performance.
