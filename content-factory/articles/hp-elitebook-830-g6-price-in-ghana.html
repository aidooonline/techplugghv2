Performance: what the numbers mean in real use

The Core i5 in the 830 G6 posts a PassMark CPU Mark of about 6,030 (i5-8265U) to 6,216 (i5-8365U), with single-thread scores around 2,095 to 2,178 and a Geekbench 5 multi-core result near 2,800. In plain terms, that places it in the upper-middle of all laptop processors ever tested, roughly 542nd out of more than 1,600 laptop chips. It is not the newest silicon, but four cores and eight threads is enough headroom for the work most people in Ghana actually do on a laptop.

In real use, the 830 G6 comfortably handles Windows 11, the full Microsoft Office suite, 15 to 25 open browser tabs (more with a 16GB upgrade), Zoom and Microsoft Teams video calls, WhatsApp and email all day, VS Code and web development, accounting software such as QuickBooks or Tally, and 1080p video playback and light photo editing in Lightroom or Photoshop. The 256GB NVMe SSD means the laptop boots to the Windows desktop in well under 20 seconds and opens apps almost instantly, which is the single biggest day-to-day speed difference you feel versus an old hard-drive laptop.

Where it is not the right tool: modern AAA gaming, 4K video rendering, large-scale 3D or CAD work, and training machine-learning models. For those, look at the HP ZBook 15 G5 workstation instead. For everything else in a normal work or study day, the 830 G6 has more than enough power.
