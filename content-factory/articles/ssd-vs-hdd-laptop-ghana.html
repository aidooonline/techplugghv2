Why the SSD matters more than the processor

People often obsess over the processor when buying a laptop, but on everyday tasks the storage drive is what you actually feel. An older laptop with a fast Core i5 and a spinning HDD will feel slow: long boot times, apps that hang, a spinning cursor. Put an SSD in that same machine and it feels new. That is because most of the waiting you do, starting up, opening Word, loading a browser, is the drive reading data, and an SSD does that many times faster than an HDD. This is why an 8th-generation EliteBook with an SSD still feels quick in 2026.

What about storage size?

A 256GB SSD, the standard on our EliteBooks, is plenty for Windows, Office, your files and everyday apps. If you store a lot of video or large files, a 512GB unit like the ZBook 15 G5 gives more room. The rule holds either way: a smaller SSD beats a larger HDD for everyday speed. Do not be tempted by a used laptop advertising a big 500GB or 1TB drive if that drive is a slow HDD.
