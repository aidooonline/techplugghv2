What the difference actually means

The EliteBook is HP's flagship business range. It uses a full aluminium chassis, carries the widest set of security and manageability features, and is the line large corporations standardise on. The ProBook sits one step below: still a business laptop, still durable, but with a slightly simpler build and a lower price. In Ghana the ProBooks in this supply run AMD Ryzen 5 processors, which are strong multi-core performers for the money.

Performance: Intel Core i5 vs AMD Ryzen 5

On raw benchmarks the Ryzen 5 in the ProBook often posts a higher PassMark multi-core score (around 7,000 to 8,000) than the 8th-generation Core i5 in the EliteBook (around 6,000). In everyday use, though, both feel very similar: Windows 11, Office, browsing, video calls and accounting software run smoothly on either, because the SSD does most of the heavy lifting for responsiveness. The Ryzen advantage shows most in sustained multi-threaded tasks; the Intel EliteBook counters with a more premium build and, for many buyers, stronger resale value.
